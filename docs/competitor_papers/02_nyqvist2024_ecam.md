# Nyqvist, R., Peltokorpi, A., & Seppänen, O. (2024): "Can ChatGPT exceed humans in construction project risk management?"

**Venue:** Engineering, Construction and Architectural Management, Vol. 31, No. 13, pp. 223–243.
DOI 10.1108/ECAM-08-2023-0819. CC BY 4.0, open access.
**Source used:** Full text (publisher's version of record, 65,000+ characters), read directly from
Aalto University's institutional repository copy (an authorized CC BY reprint of the Emerald-published
article). This is the strongest-sourced paper of the five reviewed.
**Cite key in this project's `references.bib`:** `ecam2024chatgpt_exceed_humans`

## Method
Mixed-methods, blind comparison. A single simulated construction-project-risk-management (CPRM)
scenario was given to both 16 human risk-management professionals and ChatGPT (GPT-4, April 2023
version). The resulting risk-management plans were then scored by 19 separate expert peer
reviewers, blind to authorship (they did not know which plans were human- vs AI-written).

## Sample size
- 16 human participants producing risk-management plans for the test scenario.
- 19 separate expert peer reviewers scoring the plans (anonymous, blind).
- 1 AI model (ChatGPT GPT-4, April 2023), 1 scenario, 1 output per source; not multiple prompting
  strategies or multiple runs.

## Evaluation design
Blind anonymous peer review with quantitative scoring (not stated as a fixed Likert scale in the
sections read, but produces a comparable average score) plus qualitative commentary. Headline
result: ChatGPT averaged 8.6, humans averaged 5.7; ChatGPT scored **higher**, but reviewers found
its strategies "lack practicality and specificity," where human expertise excelled.

## Stated limitations (§5.3, quoted/paraphrased directly from the paper)
1. Small sample: 16 test participants + 19 reviewers, all Finnish construction professionals;
   limits generalizability.
2. Participant expertise/motivation varied; a simulated (not real-world) scenario may have
   affected engagement differently than a real project would.
3. Only one AI model/version (GPT-4, April 2023) was used for the actual blind test; a later
   informal author-only comparison against December 2023 GPT-4 responses was not re-blinded or
   re-reviewed.
4. Only one project type/scenario was tested; findings may not generalize to other project types.
5. Efforts to equalize base knowledge across participants, plus AI's inherent limitations, could
   still have affected results.
6. Blind assessment does not fully eliminate reviewer subjectivity.

## How this study differs
Nyqvist's headline finding (ChatGPT quantitatively *beats* humans on a single simulated scenario)
is a genuinely different result from most of the other four papers here (which find ChatGPT
"moderate" or weaker); worth flagging in Related Work as a real point of tension in the
literature, not just another data point confirming the same pattern. Both studies share a
"strong-on-generic / weak-on-specific" sub-finding, but their headline verdicts differ. This
project's design closes the same gaps Nyqvist itself names: single scenario → 21 real projects;
single model → 3 models; no formal inter-rater statistic → Fleiss' kappa across 3–5 raters.
**Note for main.tex:** Nyqvist's own design is explicitly, proudly blind ("a pioneering
blind-review study"; their words). If the paper's gap table row "Non-blind rating designs
criticized in prior work" is read as characterizing all five closest-competitor papers, it would
misdescribe this one. Recommend tightening that row's wording (e.g., to specify absence of a
formal inter-rater agreement statistic like kappa, which Nyqvist's blind design does not report)
or adding a footnote acknowledging Nyqvist's blind design specifically.
