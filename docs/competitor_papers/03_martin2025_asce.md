## Method
Mixed-methods, concurrent triangulation design (Creswell & Clark, 2017), combining a structured
quantitative questionnaire with semi-structured qualitative interviews, applied to a single
infrastructure construction case study. Three participant groups: Group 1 (construction/PM
personnel using ChatGPT to assess risk), Group 2 (industry professionals using traditional
methods), Group 3 (senior experts who evaluated the outputs of Groups 1 and 2).

## Sample size
11 participants total across the three groups (purposive sampling), interviewed via
semi-structured interviews; a structured quantitative questionnaire supplemented this with scored
metrics (risk-identification accuracy, mitigation-strategy suitability, completion time,
problem-solving effectiveness) against predefined criteria — single case study, single AI tool
(ChatGPT).

## Evaluation design
Triangulated: quantitative scores (accuracy, suitability, efficiency) benchmarked against
predefined criteria, plus qualitative interview themes, plus a senior-expert evaluation layer
(Group 3) judging the outputs of the other two groups. No comparison against a real, independently
published risk register — evaluation is against expert judgment and predefined scoring criteria
for one case study.

## Stated limitations
The paper does not have a separately labeled "Limitations" section; limitations are woven into the
Conclusion and Recommendations section as forward-looking gaps. Directly from that section:
- Future work should use "more diverse samples" and "more real-world applications" — a direct
  acknowledgment that this study's 11-participant, single-case-study sample is narrow.
- "Comparative evaluation of outcomes for different generative AI models could prove useful" —
  a direct acknowledgment that only one AI model (ChatGPT) was tested here.
- AI does not yet visibly account for "cognitive biases and decision-making tendencies" specific
  to a company's risk appetite or financial position.
- "Sense making and logical intuitive reasoning" remain hard for AI to replicate — a limitation
  the authors treat as still open, not solved by this study.
